Before: MOORE, CLAY, and NALBANDIAN, Circuit Judges.
NALBANDIAN, Circuit Judge. ApplianceSmart, Inc., ran a store in Trustees Main/270,
LLC’s (Trustees Main) shopping center until 2022. JanOne, Inc. (ApplianceSmart’s former parent
company), guaranteed the lease. ApplianceSmart defaulted three times. Everyone came together
and resolved the first two breaches amicably. But the third prompted Trustees Main to repossess
the property, sue for damages, and search for a new tenant. It found a new tenant, but only after
rejecting tenants proposed by JanOne (and a related entity, Live Ventures, Inc.) that would’ve
allegedly started paying rent sooner than Trustees Main’s selected replacement. On that basis,
ApplianceSmart argues that Trustees Main didn’t reasonably mitigate its damages, warranting
reversal of the district court’s judgment in Trustees Main’s favor. But because Ohio law doesn’t
require lessors to mitigate damages by continuing to deal with demonstrably unreliable parties, we
affirm.

I.
This dispute is over a commercial lease. The only appellate question is whether the trial
court erred in finding that the lessor—the non-breaching party here—sufficiently mitigated its
damages. The court held a bench trial on that issue, where it heard the facts as told below.
Trustees Main owns a shopping center in Columbus, Ohio. That shopping center comprises
multiple retail spaces, including a “big box” space—a “larger” retail space suitable only for an
“[e]xtremely limited” pool of “national retailers.” R.92, Trial Tr., PageID 519, 540–41.
ApplianceSmart, Inc., ran a store of the same name in Trustees Main’s big-box space from
2008 until it came to a rocky end in 2022. Toward the end of its tenure, ApplianceSmart repeatedly
defaulted on the lease, ultimately leading Trustees Main to concurrently: (1) exercise its right
under the lease to repossess the property and begin looking for a replacement tenant, and (2) seek
damages against ApplianceSmart and JanOne, Inc (the guarantor on the lease and
ApplianceSmart’s previous parent company). We’ll explain ApplianceSmart’s defaults and
Trustees Main’s remediation in turn.
A.
To understand the trajectory of this case, it helps to trace the evolution of ApplianceSmart’s
and Trustees Main’s lease agreement. That evolution is largely defined by two driving forces:
changes in ApplianceSmart’s corporate structure and ownership, and ApplianceSmart’s repeated
defaults.
Start with the lease’s evolution until ApplianceSmart’s first default. The lease originally
stood between ApplianceSmart’s predecessor in interest, Appliance Recycling Centers of
America, Inc. (ARCA), and Trustees Main’s predecessor. The following years saw changes to
both the parties’ corporate structures and to the lease itself. First, ARCA shifted roles from tenant

Also in 2019, JanOne sold ApplianceSmart to a different company, Live Ventures, Inc.
But Live Ventures never took over as guarantor on the lease, leaving that role to JanOne.
